3.4. Training and Inference of RDS
Training.Our training pipeline can be roughly divided
into 2 stages: i) I-LoRA identification and ii) Dynamic com-
ponents fine-tuning. Specifically, we first split off a small
subset of the training set and run inference on these data us-
ing the SAM2 backbone, locating the most important blocks
as in Equation 11. LoRA adapters are then inserted into all
weight matrices of these blocks following Equation 3.

tune our router and the LoRA adapters jointly. Unlike tradi-

4. Experiments
4.1. Experiment Settings
Our experiments cover two tasks: Video Object Segmenta-
tion (VOS) and Video Object Tracking (VOT).
Datasets.For the VOS task, the model is evaluated in a
semi-supervised setting, where the ground-truth mask for
the first frame is provided. We adopt three main datasets
for this evaluation: DA VIS 2017 [28], SA-V [32], and
YouTube-VOS (YTVOS 2019) [43]. For VOT experiments,
we evaluate our method using the following datasets: La-
SOT [7], TrackingNet [27], and GOT-10k [18].
Implementation Details.We fine-tune the model on
DA VIS 2017 [28] with I-LoRA for 40 epochs on a single
RTX A5000 ADA, following SAM2 fine-tuning for other
settings. I-LoRA employs rank 32, applied to half of en-
coder blocks (i.e., 24 blocks) selected via the importance
metric. For evaluation, we adopt semi-supervised VOS set-
ting, where ground-truth masks are provided for the first
frame.
The baseline and default settings in the experiments are
highlighted using grey and blue , respectively.
