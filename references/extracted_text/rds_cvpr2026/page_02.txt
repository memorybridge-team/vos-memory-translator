The dynamic activation decision actually reflects an in-
sight that the importance of blocks is different and there
may exist a core subset of blocks that are fundamentally
more important across diverse samples. Motivated by this
observation, we propose an importance-aware low-rank
adaptation (I-LoRA) method, an efficient training approach
that updates parameters exclusively within these important
blocks. Specifically, it employs a dual-perspective impor-
tance metric, evaluating each block’s significance based on
its impact on the final output and its influence on the current
feature map. This design complements RDS, enabling a
substantial reduction in the adaptation costs of our dynamic
framework while preserving competitive performance.
In summary, our contributions are summarized as:
• We propose theRecurrentDynamicSubmodel (RDS)
framework. Its prediction-aware router adopts previous
predictions and current inputs to dynamically select a sub-
set of blocks per frame, overcoming the input-agnostic
nature of static approaches and eliminating the overhead
of per-block routers in dynamic methods.
• We introduce the I-LoRA strategy, which employs a dual
importance metric to identify the most vital blocks, then
selectively allocates trainable parameters to these blocks,
thus significantly lowering adaptation costs.
• Extensive empirical validation on multiple challenging
VOS and VOT benchmarks demonstrates our method
achieves significant real-world acceleration. For exam-
ple, it achieves a1.3×speedup on the DA VIS 2017 [28]
dataset with less than0.4%performance degradation,
while introducing only3%trainable parameters and re-
quiring minimal SAM2 training data.
• Additional experiments on DINOv3 [33] further demon-
strate the generalizability of our framework across differ-
ent vision foundation models.
2. Related Work
2.1. Video Object Segmentation and Tracking
Video Object Segmentation (VOS) [8, 23, 46, 52] and
Video Object Tracking (VOT) [3, 46, 50] are fundamental
and closely related areas in computer vision. While VOS
aims for pixel-level target segmentation and VOT focuses
on bounding-box localization, both require robust identity
modeling across frames to handle challenges like occlusion
and deformation.
Driven by recent advancements, state-of-the-art methods
in both fields increasingly rely on large foundation mod-
els [9, 32, 52]. Despite their eminent zero-shot generaliza-
tion and tracking performance, these heavy architectures in-
cur prohibitive computational overhead, hindering their de-
ployment in real-time video applications. Although recent
efforts have actively explored accelerating and lightening
these foundation models (e.g., via knowledge distillation or
efficient network designs) [5, 40, 41, 49], balancing perfor-
mance and efficiency remains a critical bottleneck. Cru-
cially, these lightweight variants often employ static archi-
tectures, meaning they allocate uniform computational re-
sources to every frame. This ignores the inherent temporal
redundancy and varying complexity of video streams.
To address this, we propose Recurrent Dynamic Sub-
model, which achieves faster inference and lower adap-
tation cost by dynamically skipping encoder blocks, fine-
tuning only LoRA adapters assigned to critical blocks.
